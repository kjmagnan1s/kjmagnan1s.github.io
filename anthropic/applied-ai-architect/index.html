Why Anthropic

I took an oath to the Constitution before I took a job in AI. That oath still governs my work.

For five years I have led Slalom's Justice and Public Safety Practice. I keep making the same argument inside agency rooms: public servants and the systems they deploy answer to the same document. Anthropic is the only AI lab whose technical foundation matches how government has to defend its decisions. For state and local government, Constitutional AI is not an analogy. It is the only architecture a CIO can defend in front of a city council, a federal monitor, or a judge.

I have already done this work. I have walked Slalom away from clients pushing facial recognition and mass-tracking databases when their data governance could not survive a FOIA request. I built our government AI enablement around one rule: everything we deploy has to be explainable, because "the AI told me so" is not an answer a public servant can give to a citizen or a court. The Violence Reduction Dashboard we built with the City of Chicago was designed against exactly that test.

Most SLG sales motions land at low-risk agencies and grow from there. I'd flip it. Prove the safety story at the hardest case, public safety, and the rest of government follows.

Police, courts, corrections, child welfare. Public safety with bad AI is the worst thing AI can do in this country. The DMV is not.

As Applied AI Architect I would sit next to the SLG CIO as their technical advisor. The evals I would help them build would measure constitutional and mission outcomes, not just model performance. The patterns I would see across agencies are the ones Anthropic's Product and Engineering teams need to hear about.

Additional Information

The work that survives the paperwork is the work that matters. A DMV clerk handing a sixteen-year-old their first license. A social worker who finally gets to call a family back instead of writing the case note. A patrol officer doing community policing instead of typing CAD entries. Anthropic's own labour-market work showed augmentation outpacing displacement across government, and the part that did not get enough coverage is structural: AI earns its place where it strips the documentation tax that keeps public servants away from the people they serve.

I have built my practice on a hard ranking. Get AI right for the highest-stakes agencies first, and the easier wins fall into place. Police, courts, corrections, child welfare, emergency response. These are the agencies where bad AI is catastrophic and where constitutional design is the only kind that survives outside review.

The other half of the work is teaching. I have trained 100+ public sector staff on what to ask an AI vendor before they sign a contract. That is what scales Claude from a single agency rollout to a statewide operating model.

I want to do this work at Anthropic because the technology matches the duty. I want to bring the agencies along.
