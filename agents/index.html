6 side businesses an AI agent can run for you

These ideas started from Chris Koerner's video "I Let AI Make and Save Me Money for 6 Weeks" (The Koerner Office podcast, October 21, 2026) and his post on X. The matchmaker idea comes from Greg Isenberg's Startup Ideas Podcast episode "Meta Muse Will Create 1,000 Millionaires" (October 19, 2026). The numbers are from those sources. The prompts and the takes are mine.

By Kevin Magnan · @vibewithkevin

"Agents do the clicking and the work now. You just need to find the underserved business angle."

Kevin

I think the hard part moved. The clicking is cheap now. Finding a niche nobody's serving well is the whole game.

So each idea below is a pattern, with a few niches you could drop in, and a prompt you can paste into a browser agent tonight. Pick the niche closest to work you already know. That's where you'll spot the angle first.

Before you paste these
  • Use a browser agent: ChatGPT agent mode, Claude in Chrome, Meta's Muse, or something like them. A plain chat window can't click for you.
  • Run the research part first. Every prompt starts with research only. Nothing gets sent or bought.
  • You approve before anything goes out. The agent drafts the messages, forms, and carts, shows you, and waits for you to say "go".
  • Expect some manual steps. Agents don't stay running for days, most can't make phone calls, and they stop at CAPTCHAs, logins, and checkout. The agent puts those on a "do by hand" list. When a prompt has a Run 2, paste it yourself later (or set it as a scheduled task if your agent has those).
  • For anything on Facebook, use Meta's own agent or do it by hand. Running Facebook with an outside agent can break its rules and get your account flagged.

1. The quote agent for big projects

You collect a stack of real price quotes for a big job, so the owner doesn't spend weeks chasing contractors.

Who pays youHomeowners and business owners with a big job coming: a roof, a kitchen, a pool, fleet repairs, a commercial cleaning contract. Pool consultants charge 3 to 6% of the job for this work.
The proofOne guy wanted 10 quotes on a pool. The old way took 2 weeks, 4 companies, and got zero quotes. An agent got 8 quotes in 24 hours, from $120,000 to $240,000 for the same pool.

That spread is the business. Same pool, double the price. Anyone planning a big job will pay to see that before they sign.

Run 1 · research, then send
I need ballpark price quotes for a [PROJECT, e.g. new roof, kitchen remodel, pool, fleet brake jobs, office cleaning contract] in [CITY OR AREA]. Use the web browser and work step by step.

The project: [SPECS: size, materials, must-haves]. I've attached [PLANS, PHOTOS, OR A SHORT DESCRIPTION].

Part 1: research. Don't contact anyone yet.
1. Search Google and Google Maps for [TYPE OF COMPANY, e.g. roofing contractor] that serve [CITY OR AREA]. Find at least [NUMBER, e.g. 20].
2. Make a spreadsheet called "[PROJECT] quotes" with these columns: Company, Website, Phone, Email, Contact method (form or email), Date contacted, Replied (yes/no), Quote amount, Follow-up sent, Do by hand, Notes.
3. Fill in this message with my details:
   "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME]. I'm planning a [PROJECT] in [CITY]. Here are the specs: [SPECS]. I just want a ballpark, I won't hold you to it. You can reach me at [EMAIL] or [PHONE]."
4. Stop. Show me the company list and the message, and wait for me to say "go".

Part 2: send (only after I say go).
5. For each company I approve, fill out the contact form on its website with the message. Attach [PHOTO] if the form allows it. If there's no form, email the message from [EMAIL ACCOUNT].
6. If a form has a CAPTCHA or wants me to log in or make an account, skip it. Mark it "do by hand" and move on.
7. Log every company as you go, including any you couldn't reach and why.
8. Don't agree to anything, book anything, pay anything, or share any details beyond what's in the message.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of how many companies you contacted, which ones I need to do by hand, and anything that looked off.
Run 2 · paste in 3 days
This is the follow-up for my "[PROJECT] quotes" spreadsheet. Work step by step.

1. Check [EMAIL INBOX] for replies from the companies in the spreadsheet. Add each quote amount and mark Replied.
2. For every company that hasn't replied, draft one short follow-up: "Hi, just following up on my ballpark request for a [PROJECT] in [CITY]. Any rough number helps."
3. Stop. Show me the drafts and wait for me to say "go". Then send them the same way you sent the first message, and fill in the Follow-up sent column.

Hand back: the updated spreadsheet, plus a short summary of how many replied, the lowest, highest, and middle quote, and anything that looked strange.

An agent can't sit around for 3 days waiting on replies. Paste Run 2 yourself, or set it as a scheduled task if your agent has those. To make this a business, run it for clients: their project, their contact details, and a flat fee or a small percent of the job.

2. The garage machine

You photograph someone's unwanted stuff. The agent prices it, writes the listings, and drafts the buyer replies. You sell it.

Who pays youPeople downsizing or clearing out a parent's house, families stuck with estate leftovers, and small businesses dumping old equipment. Consignment shops charge 30 to 50% to do this. You become the consignment shop with no shop.
The ruleEvery day an item gets 3 inquiries or less, drop the price 10%. It works on the buy side too. One guy had an agent find spray foam contractors within 30 miles. First quote back: $1,200, against $12,000 the old way.
Facebook stepsMarketplace is where this stuff sells, and Facebook doesn't like outside bots. Post the listings and send the replies yourself, or use Meta's own agent for those steps. Any browser agent can do the pricing and writing.
Run 1 · price and write
I want to sell the items in the attached photos. Work step by step. Don't post or message anything.

1. For each photo, figure out what the item is, the brand if you can tell, and its condition.
2. Look up eBay sold listings for the same or similar items, plus Craigslist near [CITY]. Set a starting price that should sell within about a week. Never go below my floor price: [FLOOR PRICE, or "50% of the starting price"].
3. Write a listing for each item: a clear title, an honest description using only what you can see and my notes ([NOTES, e.g. "the dresser has a scratch on the left side"]), the category, and the price. Pickup area: [GENERAL AREA, e.g. a neighborhood or cross streets].
4. If a site has a CAPTCHA or wants a login, skip it and note it.
5. Make a spreadsheet called "Sale items" with these columns: Item, Photo, Starting price, Floor price, Current price, Listing title, Listing text, Date listed, Listing link, Inquiries today, Status, Buyer, Pickup time, Sold price.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of the total starting value and any items you weren't sure how to price.

Post the listings on Marketplace (by hand, or with Meta's agent). Then paste this once each evening.

Run 2 · each evening
Daily check-in for my "Sale items" spreadsheet. Work step by step.

1. Read today's buyer messages. If you're Meta's agent, read them in my Marketplace inbox. Otherwise, use the messages I pasted at the bottom.
2. Draft a reply to each one using only what's in the listing and my notes. If you don't know an answer, flag it for me.
3. Negotiate politely in the drafts. Accept any offer at or above the current price. Counter anything below it. Never go under the floor price.
4. Only share [PICKUP ADDRESS] after a buyer confirms a day, a time, and a price. Offer pickup times only from: [MY PICKUP WINDOWS]. Payment is [CASH, or a payment app you name] at pickup only.
5. Don't draft a reply to anyone who asks for shipping, offers a check, overpays, or asks for a verification code. Flag them as likely scams.
6. Stop. Show me the drafts. If you're Meta's agent, send only the ones I approve after I say "go". Otherwise I'll send them myself.
7. Update the spreadsheet. If an item got 3 inquiries or less today, drop its Current price 10%, but not below the floor, and list the price changes I need to make.

Hand back: the updated spreadsheet, plus a short summary of what sold, for how much, which pickups are booked, and which messages need me.

Flip it for the buy side: have the agent find [SERVICE] contractors within 30 miles, draft the quote requests, and show you before anything goes out.

3. Dropshipping in messy niches

You sell stuff that's hard to shop for online. The agent finds the cheapest supplier, and you list it on a clean site for more.

Who pays youShoppers in messy niches with no Amazon-style catalog, where prices are all over the place. Think used restaurant equipment, painting and contractor gear (sprayers, scaffolding, pressure washers), or discontinued replacement parts for appliances, furnaces, and tractors. You list 30 to 60% above your cost.
An exampleSay a customer pays you about $60 to $70 for a fruit tree. The agent buys it from a supplier for $34 and ships it to them. (That's Chris's example, and it's the whole model.)

Pick a niche you already know. If you run a crew, you know which brands people trust and what a fair price looks like, and that's half the research done.

Run 1 · research only
I want to sell [NICHE, e.g. used restaurant equipment, paint sprayers, discontinued furnace parts] online without holding inventory. Use the web browser and work step by step. Don't buy anything or sign up for anything.

1. Find at least [NUMBER, e.g. 15] suppliers that sell [NICHE] and ship to customers in [COUNTRY OR REGION].
2. For the [NUMBER, e.g. 10] most popular products in this niche, find each supplier's price, shipping cost, delivery time, and return policy.
3. If a supplier site has a CAPTCHA or hides prices behind a login, skip it and mark it "do by hand".
4. Make a spreadsheet called "[NICHE] products" with these columns: Product, Supplier, Supplier link, Supplier price, Shipping cost, Total cost, Delivery time, Return policy, Lowest price I found elsewhere, My price, Do by hand, Notes.
5. Set My price at 30 to 60% above Total cost, and check that it's still fair next to the lowest price you found elsewhere.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary with the top 10 products ranked by profit per sale and any supplier that looks unreliable.
Run 2 · when orders come in
New orders came in on my store. Work step by step.

1. Open [WHERE ORDERS SHOW UP, e.g. my Shopify admin] and find orders that aren't in the "Orders" tab of my "[NICHE] products" spreadsheet yet.
2. For each one, go to the supplier from the spreadsheet and add the same item to the cart, shipping to the buyer's name and address.
3. Stop and tell me if the supplier price is higher than the spreadsheet, the item is out of stock, or the order is over [DOLLAR LIMIT].
4. Stop at checkout. Show me the supplier, price, shipping, and total, and wait for me to confirm. I'll enter payment myself. Don't save any card details.
5. After I've paid, add the order to the "Orders" tab with: Date, Buyer, Product, What they paid, What I paid, Supplier order number, Tracking number.
6. Draft the shipping email to the buyer with the tracking number if there is one. Show me before you send it.

Hand back: the updated Orders tab, plus a short summary of orders placed, profit, and anything that needs me.

Run 2 is a fresh paste each time orders come in, or once a day. Tracking numbers often show up a day later, so the next run can fill those in.

Before you list anythingCheck each supplier yourself and read their return policy. When something arrives broken, the buyer comes to you.

4. The agent matchmaker

The agent finds people offering hard-to-find help in public groups and posts. You check them out, and you run the agency.

Who pays youFamilies or businesses who'd otherwise pay an agency's placement fee, or who can't find the help at all. Think night nurses, house cleaners, tutors, mobile mechanics. Your margin is high because there are no recruiters to pay.
Real exampleOne family had a baby and couldn't find a night nurse. The agency they tried admitted it found nurses by searching Facebook groups, like Miami or South Florida night nurse groups. So the dad told an AI agent to find night nurses around $30 an hour in Montreal from public Facebook groups. It came back with a list.

Then you check out the people on the list (call it cleaning the data), price it, and run the agency. You can give the agent its own phone number, about $15 for an agent phone plan through a voice AI service, so it can call people. More niches from the same story: night nurses in Tampa, furniture in Austin, plumbers in Phoenix. There are 3 levels: find the people, own the cleaned list, then own the room, meaning the Facebook group or niche page itself.

Run 1 · build the list
I'm building a list of [ROLE, e.g. night nurses, house cleaners, tutors, mobile mechanics] who offer their services in [CITY] at around [RATE, e.g. $30 an hour]. Use the web browser and work step by step. Don't message anyone.

1. Search public posts and listings for [ROLE] offering services in [CITY]. If you're Meta's agent, include public Facebook groups and Marketplace, with group names like "[CITY] [ROLE]" and "[REGION] [ROLE]". Otherwise, skip Facebook and use Google, Craigslist services listings, and other public pages.
2. Collect only information people posted publicly themselves. Don't guess at or look up private details. If a page has a CAPTCHA or needs a login, skip it and note it.
3. Make a spreadsheet called "[ROLE] [CITY] list" with these columns: Name, Profile link, Rate, Experience, Availability, Where I found them, Duplicate (yes/no), Looks fake (yes/no), Approved (yes/no), Notes.
4. Log every person as you go. Mark duplicates. If a profile or post looks fake (no history, copied text, a rate far off from everyone else), mark it and say why in Notes.
5. Stop when the list is done. Don't message, call, friend, or comment on anyone.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of how many people you found, how many look solid, the rate range, and anything that looked strange.
Run 2 · after you approve people
I've filled in the Approved column in my "[ROLE] [CITY] list" spreadsheet. Work step by step.

1. For each approved person, write a short, friendly intro message. Use this outline: who I am ([YOUR NAME]), that I saw their post in [WHERE I FOUND THEM], that I'm looking for [ROLE] in [CITY] for [SCHEDULE OR JOB], and how to reach me at [PHONE OR EMAIL]. Make each one personal to their post.
2. Put each draft in Notes.
3. Stop. Show me the drafts and wait for me to say "go". Send only the ones I approve, and no more than [NUMBER, e.g. 5] a day. If they're on Facebook and you're not Meta's agent, leave them for me to send by hand.
4. Add a Contacted date for each message sent.

Hand back: the updated spreadsheet, plus a short summary of who got a message and who's waiting on me.
Before you place anyoneCheck references and any license or background check the role needs, like for childcare or nursing. Follow each group's rules.

To turn this into a business, pick one role in one city, clean the list, and charge the families or businesses who need the help. Then grow the group or page where those people gather.

5. The outreach machine for AI setup clients

You find good local businesses that lose customers to missed calls and slow booking, then show them a fix before you ask for anything.

Who pays youGood-but-sloppy local businesses: great reviews, nobody picking up the phone. Pet groomers, auto shops, salons, cleaners. They pay you to set up an AI receptionist, a missed-call text-back, and a booking page.
The filterOne niche, one city. Keep businesses with 4+ stars, 50+ reviews, no booking link, and an owner who never replies to reviews. Then test what happens after hours.

After the list comes the pitch: a scored one-page audit, a 3 to 5 minute Loom video walking through it, then a working demo. Free Loom covers videos up to 5 minutes, so keep it under that if you're on the free plan.

Run 1 · find and test
Help me find local businesses that need help answering calls and booking customers. The niche is [NICHE, e.g. pet groomers, auto repair shops, hair salons] in [CITY]. Use the web browser and work step by step.

Part 1: research. Don't contact anyone yet.
1. Search Google Maps for [NICHE] in [CITY]. Go through every page of results.
2. Keep only the businesses that have all four of these:
   - 4 stars or higher
   - 50 or more reviews
   - No online booking link on their Google listing or their website
   - The owner hasn't replied to any recent reviews
3. For each one, note the hours on their Google listing and whether the website has a contact form.
4. Make a spreadsheet called "[NICHE] [CITY] leads" with these columns: Business, Owner name (if listed), Phone, Website, Stars, Review count, Hours, Booking link (yes/no), Owner replies (yes/no), Contact form (yes/no), Form sent, Reply time, After-hours call result, Audit score, Do by hand, Notes.
5. Draft this short question for their contact forms: "Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME]. Quick question: if I wanted to book [SERVICE] next week, what's the fastest way to get on your schedule?"
6. Stop. Show me the list and the draft, and wait for me to say "go".

Part 2: test (only after I say go).
7. Send the question through the contact form of each business I approve, and log the date and time in Form sent. If a form has a CAPTCHA or needs a login, skip it and mark it "do by hand".
8. Optional, only if you have a phone or voice tool connected: after [TIME, e.g. 7 pm] their local time, call each business once and note whether it goes to voicemail. Hang up without leaving a message. If you can't make calls, leave that column blank.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of how many businesses passed the filter, how many forms went out, and which ones I need to do by hand.
Run 2 · paste in 2 days
This is the follow-up for my "[NICHE] [CITY] leads" spreadsheet. Work step by step. Don't contact any business.

1. Check [EMAIL INBOX] for replies to the contact forms. For each business, fill in Reply time (how long they took) or "no reply".
2. For the top [NUMBER, e.g. 10] businesses, write a one-page audit scored out of 100. Score these: how easy it is to book, what happens to an after-hours call or form, whether reviews get answered, and how the website works on a phone. For each low score, write one plain sentence on what it costs them, like "a form sent Monday got no reply by Wednesday, so that customer probably booked somewhere else."
3. For each of those businesses, write a short outline I can follow while recording a 3 to 5 minute screen-share video walking through their audit.
4. For each one, write the demo I'll build: the greeting their AI receptionist would use, the text a missed caller would get, and the services and times their booking page would show.
5. Draft a short first email to each owner that links to their audit video. Don't send it. I'll send these myself, a few at a time.

Hand back: the updated spreadsheet with an Audit score for each, plus the top 10 audits, outlines, demos, and email drafts, best first.
One ruleCold calls and texts have rules that differ by state and country. Check yours before you contact anyone.

6. The $11 website

You find a city where people search for a service but few businesses show up, build a simple site that ranks for it, and rent the leads to one local company.

Who pays youOne local business that wants every lead from your site. Example price: $800 a month, raised to $1,200 later. Look for weird local services with weak Google Maps competition: crane rental, shower door repair, septic pumping, mobile welding. Skip dentists, HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and electrical, because everyone already sells to them. Go wide and go weird.
The proofA tool showed Laredo, Texas needed crane rental. An agent bought the domain for $11 and built the site. Within a month: 3 leads, for jobs of about $3,000, $23,000, and $9,000.

The first leads went free to basically the only crane company in Laredo with a web presence. The plan is to make the site exclusive to one business. Run the prompt in 2 parts. Part A only researches. Part B spends money, so run it after you've picked a winner. The agent stops at checkout and waits for your OK.

Part A · research only
Help me find a local service where people search on Google but few companies show up. Use the web browser and work step by step. Don't buy anything or sign up for anything that costs money.

1. List [NUMBER, e.g. 30] unusual local services that businesses or homeowners hire, like crane rental, shower door repair, septic pumping, or mobile welding. Skip dentists, HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and electrical. Add a few of my own: [AN EXAMPLE OR TWO OF YOURS].
2. Pick [NUMBER, e.g. 20] cities in [STATE OR REGION] with at least [POPULATION, e.g. 100,000] people.
3. For each service and city pair, search Google Maps for "[SERVICE] in [CITY]". Count how many real businesses show up, how many have a website, and how many reviews the top three have.
4. Check whether people search for it, using [GOOGLE TRENDS, GOOGLE KEYWORD PLANNER, OR A TOOL YOU USE]. Note the search numbers or trend you see. If a tool needs a login or shows a CAPTCHA, skip it and tell me.
5. Make a spreadsheet called "Rank and rent research" with these columns: Service, City, Businesses on Maps, Businesses with a website, Top 3 review counts, Search demand, Evidence link, Notes.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of the top 5 pairs with the most search demand and the weakest competition. For each one, show the evidence: the counts, the links, and the search numbers.
Part B · after you approve one
I approved [SERVICE] in [CITY]. Build a simple website for it, step by step.

1. Find an available domain name that includes the service and the city, like [SERVICE][CITY].com. Show me 3 options and wait for me to pick one.
2. Add the domain I picked to the cart at [REGISTRAR, e.g. Cloudflare or Namecheap]. Stop at checkout, show me the total, and wait for me to confirm. I'll enter payment myself. Don't save any card details. If it costs more than [DOLLAR LIMIT, e.g. $15], tell me first.
3. Build a one-page site on [SITE BUILDER, e.g. Carrd or Google Sites]. Include: a clear headline with the service and city, what the service is, the areas served, a phone number [PHONE NUMBER], and a quote request form that sends to [EMAIL].
4. Make it easy for Google to understand: put the service and city in the page title, the main heading, and the description. Make sure it loads fast and reads well on a phone.
5. Set up Google Analytics and Google Search Console for the site, using my Google account [GOOGLE ACCOUNT], and submit the site to Search Console.
6. If any step needs a login, a verification code, or a CAPTCHA, stop and add it to a "do by hand" list.
7. Make a spreadsheet called "[SERVICE] [CITY] leads" with these columns: Date, Name, Phone, Email, Job details, Rough job size, Sent to.

Hand back: the site link, the leads spreadsheet, what it cost, and the "do by hand" list.

Dead-lead revival for local businesses

A business hands you its old estimates and form fills that never closed. The agent drafts follow-ups, you approve them, and you turn the replies into booked appointments.

Who pays youThe business. Every old lead it already paid to get is a second chance at a job.
Real pricingAgencies charge per booked appointment, around $120 each in flexxable.com's pricing example, or about $5,000 per campaign at doubletimeai.com.
Run 1 · draft, then send
I'm helping [BUSINESS NAME], a [TYPE OF BUSINESS] in [CITY], follow up with old leads that never booked. Work from the attached spreadsheet they exported: [SPREADSHEET NAME]. Work step by step.

1. Read every lead. Note their name, phone, what they originally asked for, and when.
2. Skip anyone marked do not contact, anyone who already booked, and anyone with no phone number.
3. For each remaining lead, draft one short follow-up text that mentions their original request, like: "Hi [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [BUSINESS NAME]. You asked about [ORIGINAL REQUEST] back in [MONTH]. Still need help? You can grab a time here: [CALENDAR LINK]."
4. Add a tab called "Follow-ups" with these columns: Name, Phone, Original request, Date of request, Draft text, Approved (yes/no), Sent date, Reply, Booked (yes/no), Appointment time.
5. Stop. Show me the drafts and wait for me to mark them Approved and say "go".
6. Send only the approved texts through [TEXTING TOOL], no more than [NUMBER, e.g. 25] a day, and fill in Sent date.

Hand back: the Follow-ups tab, plus a short summary of how many texts went out and how many are waiting.
Run 2 · paste each day after
Check replies for the "Follow-ups" tab for [BUSINESS NAME]. Work step by step.

1. Open [TEXTING TOOL] and log every new reply in the Reply column.
2. If someone asks to stop, mark them do not contact and never message them again.
3. If someone wants to book, draft a reply with [CALENDAR LINK]. Show me before you send it.
4. Check [CALENDAR] for new appointments from these leads and fill in Booked and Appointment time.

Hand back: the updated tab, plus a short summary of replies, appointments booked, and any reply I need to answer myself.
One ruleOnly contact people who gave the business their number, and follow the texting and calling consent rules where you and they live.

The money-finder agent

The agent hunts for money a client is losing: bills that could be lower, subscriptions nobody uses, and money they're owed.

Who pays youBusy households and small offices, for a cut of what you save them.
The sourceMeta's Alexandr Wang started the #MuseMoneyChallenge in September 2026: have an agent save you $1,000 by negotiating bills, canceling unused subscriptions, and finding money you're owed. Business Insider noted many early replies came from Meta's own staff.
Paste into your browser agent
Help me find money for [CLIENT NAME]. I've attached their statements: [STATEMENTS, e.g. 3 months of bank and credit card statements]. Work step by step. Don't cancel, pay, call, or sign up for anything.

1. List every recurring charge: who it's to, the amount, how often, and the last date charged.
2. Flag subscriptions that look unused or doubled up, and anything that went up in price.
3. For each bill that could be lower (phone, internet, insurance, [OTHER BILLS]), write a short negotiation script [CLIENT NAME] or I can use on the phone or in chat, including what competitors charge if you can find it.
4. Search the official unclaimed property site for [STATE OR STATES THEY'VE LIVED IN] for [CLIENT NAME] and [OTHER NAMES OR PAST ADDRESSES]. Use only official state sites, never a paid finder service. If a site shows a CAPTCHA, stop and mark that search "do by hand".
5. Make a spreadsheet called "[CLIENT NAME] money found" with these columns: Item, Type (cancel, negotiate, unclaimed), Current cost, Possible savings, Next step, Do by hand, Approved (yes/no), Notes.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of the total possible savings, the top 5 moves, and anything you need me to check. Wait for my approval before taking any action.

The calls are on you unless your agent has a phone or voice tool connected. Cancellations happen in a second run, after the client approves each one, and the agent stops before any final cancel button.

The class-action claim finder

The agent reads an inbox, finds class action settlements the person qualifies for, and lines up the claims.

Who pays youAnyone who's bought things online for years. One app idea: take a small cut, or charge $10 and refund it if it finds less than that.
The proofOne person connected an agent to his inbox and asked it to find settlements he was eligible for and file them. It found 8, and he's gotten about $200 back so far.
Paste into your browser agent
Help me find class action settlements I may be eligible for. I'm logged in to my inbox at [EMAIL ACCOUNT] in this browser. Work step by step. Don't file anything yet.

1. Search my inbox for settlement notices, "class action" emails, and purchase receipts from the last [NUMBER, e.g. 5] years.
2. Check official settlement administrator sites for open claims that match companies I bought from or used. Only use the official claim site named in the settlement notice or the court's site.
3. Make a spreadsheet called "Settlement claims" with these columns: Settlement, Company, Why I might qualify, Proof required, Proof I have (link to the email), Claim deadline, Estimated payout, Official claim link, Confirmed true (yes/no), Filed (yes/no), Do by hand.
4. Sort it by deadline, soonest first.
5. Stop. Show me the list and wait for me to mark each one Confirmed true.
6. For each claim I confirm, fill out the official form using [MY NAME, ADDRESS, AND PAYOUT METHOD, e.g. a PayPal email]. Stop before the final submit button, show me the filled form, and submit only after I say "go". If a form has a CAPTCHA or needs a login, mark it "do by hand".
7. Never pay a fee to file, and never share my details with a site that isn't the official claim site.

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of claims found, claims filed, deadlines in the next 30 days, and which ones I need to do by hand.
One ruleOnly file claims you actually qualify for. Filing a false claim can be fraud.

How to pick what to build

The cheat code: have the agent pull apart a company that's already winning in a niche, so you copy what already works.

Who it's forYou, before you spend a dollar on any of the ideas above.
The cheat codeLook at the company's ads in the Meta Ad Library (video or static, which countries), its backlinks, and its website year by year in the Wayback Machine, then see what changed over time.
Paste into your browser agent
I want to study [COMPANY NAME] ([WEBSITE]), a company that's winning in [NICHE]. Use the web browser and work step by step. Don't sign up for anything that costs money.

1. Search the Meta Ad Library for [COMPANY NAME]. For each active ad, note: video or static, the main hook, the offer, which countries it runs in, and how long it's been running.
2. Find who links to [WEBSITE] using [BACKLINK TOOL, e.g. a free backlink checker]. List the top 20 linking sites and what kind of site each one is. If the tool needs a login or shows a CAPTCHA, skip it and tell me.
3. Open [WEBSITE] in the Wayback Machine. Look at one snapshot per year going back [NUMBER, e.g. 5] years. For each year, note the headline, the main product or offer, the price if shown, and anything new or removed.
4. Make a spreadsheet called "[COMPANY NAME] teardown" with tabs for Ads (Ad link, Format, Hook, Offer, Countries, Running since), Backlinks (Site, Type of site), and Website history (Year, Headline, Main offer, Price, What changed).

Hand back: the spreadsheet, plus a short summary of what changed over time, what they seem to have doubled down on, and 3 ideas I could copy in [MY CITY OR MY NICHE].

Before you start

Agent apps change fast. Any browser agent that can click and fill out forms will work with these prompts. Watch what it does the first few times, and keep anything that spends money behind a yes from you.
