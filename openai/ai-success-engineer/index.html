Why OpenAI

The AI Success Engineer JD says government customers have vital missions and they must be met with serious technology. I believe the people who wrote that meant it, which is rare for a job posting and is why I am writing back.

I was a sworn officer at St. Louis County PD for two years. I left for the University of Chicago Crime Lab because I wanted to know whether anything I had been doing on patrol actually worked. I ran research partnerships with police departments and city agencies there for four years, then joined Slalom in 2021 and ended up leading their Justice and Public Safety practice. The AI work I run now lands at the same kinds of agencies I used to work for.

Last year I led a Snowflake Cortex and Claude deployment at a statewide public safety agency. Roughly thirty internal affairs reports a week, each one a multi-hour read for the leadership team that had to brief the governor's office and respond to media on high-profile incidents. We moved that read time to seconds and stood up a public dashboard on top of it. The model was the easy part. Getting the agency to trust the output, integrating it with how the team already worked, and coaching the people who would have to defend the dashboard in front of cameras was the rest of it. The pilot graduated to production because I owned that arc, not because the model itself was clever.

Once you have shipped AI inside a state public safety agency, every other enterprise customer feels like the easy version.

The rest of the JD reads like a description of my current job at a different company. Embedding with customer teams to map workflows. Building adoption roadmaps tied to executive KPIs. Running executive readouts. Surfacing field patterns back to Product. Designing workshops that turn into expansion conversations. That is what my weeks are made of at Slalom. Three net-new AI implementation projects came out of customer workshops I designed last year. I would bring all of that work with me.

I also build outside of Slalom. Five production iOS and web apps shipped under my own name on the model APIs. A weekly cohort called AI Recess where I teach non-technical people to ship real things. A daily TikTok at @vibewithkevin where I break down what I am learning that week. None of it is a hobby. Adoption is a teaching problem before it is a technology problem, and I run the teaching practice in public to stay sharp on it.

The hardest part of post-deployment is almost never the model itself. It is the operating model around the model. The data sits in silos. The processes that matter only ever made it onto paper. The agents need context that nobody has the infrastructure to give them. Closing that gap is what an AI Success Engineer is actually for, and OpenAI's customers, government and enterprise, have the most to gain from somebody who has been closing it for the last decade.

Workshops that produced net-new implementation projects. Champion programs that scaled across 100+ consultants and the customers they serve. Every artifact translates.

This is the role I have been pointing my career at without knowing it had a name.
